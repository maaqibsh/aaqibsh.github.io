Research

I work on high-power wide-bandgap converters that connect electric vehicles, batteries, renewables and the grid, with a focus on making them efficient, compact and reliable.

Current · Sep 2026 – present · U.S. Department of Energy

150 kW integrated charger-inverter for electric vehicles

Electric vehicles usually carry a traction inverter and a separate on-board charger. This project merges the two: one SiC power stage drives the motor while the car is moving and becomes the charger when it is parked. The battery feeds an isolated dual-active-bridge (DAB) converter, then a three-phase inverter with an LCL filter. The inverter output can be switched between a permanent-magnet synchronous motor, three-phase AC charging from the 208 V grid, and a separate DC charging port.

My contributions

  • Designed the DSP control board: schematic, PCB layout and component selection, including differential PWM, fault and temperature links for noise immunity and a resolver interface for motor position sensing.
  • Sized the power stage: 1200 V SiC modules, DC-link capacitor, LCL grid filter, DAB transformer and leakage inductance, and dead times for soft switching.
  • Developing DAB firmware with several phase-shift modulation schemes.
  • The system is now in testing, and I am building the dynamometer setup for inverter validation.

Completed · Jan 2025 – Aug 2026 · DOE Award DE-SC0023970

15 kW GaN quad-active-bridge converter for DC microgrids

A quad-active-bridge (QAB) converter joins four DC ports (PV, battery, EV and the DC grid) through one high-frequency multi-winding transformer, so power can move in any direction between them with galvanic isolation. This project, carried out with Epower Technology LLC, built a 15 kW, 400 V prototype with GaN transistors switching at 50–100 kHz.

My contributions

  • Designed the QAB converter, the power-stage PCB layout and the gate-driver circuitry for fast GaN switching.
  • Developed firmware for bidirectional multi-port power-flow control with single-, extended- and dual-phase-shift (SPS, EPS, DPS) modulation.
  • Carried out all prototype testing and soft-switching validation.
  • Built thermal models of the high-frequency transformer losses to support the electrothermal design.

Outputs: first-author paper at IEEE COMPEL 2025; a journal paper being finalized; one review paper submitted.

M.S. research · 2022 – 2024 · Kyungpook National University

Resonant converters, integrated magnetics and fuel-cell emulation

In the Power Electronics and Magnetics Design (PEMD) Lab, advised by Prof. Honnyong Cha, I worked on:

  • Thesis: an interleaved LLC resonant converter using a quad-winding transformer and a shared inductor.
  • Fuel-cell emulator (Korean government-sponsored): a buck-converter-based emulator that reproduces real fuel-cell I-V characteristics as hydrogen pressure, air pressure and cell temperature change.
  • Integrated magnetics for PFC: an EE-core coupled inductor with a center-gap structure for a two-phase interleaved boost PFC converter, and how the coupling affects performance.
